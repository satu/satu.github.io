Where the sun sets into Stromboli

Pick an evening. The gold line is every point from which the setting sun sits exactly on the volcano's summit; the marker is where that line meets the shore. Tap anywhere on the map to ask the other question: from here, on which evenings?

Temperature and pressure only scale the standard refraction model; the real near-horizon refraction can still differ by ±0.1° (≈ ±150 m of shoreline here). The two summits are ~630 m apart north–south, so from Calabria the ridge looks like a flat top about one sun-width wide; the midpoint is the visual centre.

The year, evening by evening

The point walks north in spring (Nicotera → Pizzo → the Gulf of Sant'Eufemia → Amantea area by the winter solstice) and back south in summer, pausing at the solstices. In late March–early May and early August–mid September it sweeps the Costa degli Dei at roughly a kilometre a day. Tap the curve to jump to that evening.

How it's computed, and how far to trust it

  • Sun position: a line-by-line port of NREL's Solar Position Algorithm (Reda & Andreas), the VSOP87-based standard with ±0.0003° accuracy, including nutation, aberration, ΔT and topocentric parallax. It was checked against the reference implementation (pvlib) at 400 random epochs: agreement to 10⁻¹¹°.
  • Geometry: WGS84 ellipsoid; the direction to the summit is the true straight line from your eye to the peak (ECEF → local horizon), so Earth curvature and the ellipsoid are exact. Summit coordinates: I Vancori 38.787569 N 15.21083 E, 924 m; Pizzo sopra la Fossa ≈ 38.7933 N 15.2110 E, 918 m (the Pizzo position is good to ~100 m, which is ±0.05° at this range).
  • Refraction: the sun's apparent altitude uses Bennett's formula (with his 1982 correction), scaled by pressure and temperature; the summit is lifted by terrestrial refraction k·d/2R. The moment is solved for the sun's true altitude that puts its refracted image on the summit's refracted image. This is the weak link: near the horizon the real atmosphere differs from any model by typically ±0.05–0.1°, which moves the shoreline point by ~±75–150 m and the time by under a minute. Calm, settled evenings behave best; a sea much warmer or colder than the air does not.
  • Coastline: GSHHS full resolution (WVS-derived, ~100–200 m fidelity); municipality names from ISTAT boundaries; locality names are approximate labels. The line itself does not depend on the coastline — trust the line over the marker if they disagree about where the water starts.
  • Dates: computed for the real calendar date, not a day-of-year average. Because of the leap cycle the same date drifts by up to ±0.3° (≈ ±700 m of shoreline on the Costa degli Dei) from year to year.
  • Not modelled: local terrain blocking the view near headlands (Capo Vaticano, Capo Cozzo, Capo Suvero), the volcano's plume, and haze.
